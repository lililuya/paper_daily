## Agent（1 篇）

### ⭐⭐⭐ ABF-Bench: A Verifiable Benchmark for Agentic Blockchain Forensics

**方向**：Agent · 👍 3 | **作者**：Xiaohang Yu, Hejia Geng, William Knottenbelt | [原文](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23656) | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23656)

<sub>英文标题：ABF-Bench: A Verifiable Benchmark for Agentic Blockchain Forensics</sub>

> Blockchain forensics is adversarial and open-ended: money launderers split, swap, and bridge funds, cut their trail at mixers or custodial services, and change tactics from one incident to the next. Investigators must keep adapting their tracing in response. Yet the immutability of public ledgers preserves every recorded movement in public view, making much of the investigation independently verifiable. We introduce ABF-Bench, an interactive benchmark that makes evidence-backed fund tracing a verifiable agent task, in which every claimed movement must cite ledger records the agent retrieved. A case-to-task pipeline builds well-posed tasks across ledgers: conservative rules halt tracing wherever the records leave a continuation unresolved, and each task's reference graph is the answer those rules determine. Built from real laundering activity, the tasks combine multi-hop transfers, DeFi exchanges, cross-chain round trips, custodial and mixer deposits, and address poisoning. We also propose the ABF agent harness, built around a working memory, and evaluate it alongside general-purpose agents and fixed-order tracers. The harness reaches 75% edge recall, against 47% for Codex with the same model and 10% for Claude Code, which loses recall to overconfidence: it cites records it left unretrieved. Compared with fixed-order tracers, the harness acquires evidence faster than the best of them early in the budget and recognizes undisclosed custodial services that they walk past (89% vs. 0%), with 98% fidelity. However, agents tend to stop early and lose recall on larger tasks, which holds their final recall below the best tracer. Beyond exposing these gaps, ABF-Bench opens blockchain forensics to LLM agents as a verifiable domain, where the verdicts that score agents also offer potential training signals.

## 大语言模型（1 篇）

### ⭐⭐⭐ Broken on Arrival: Non-Functional Quantized LLM Artifacts in Public Model Registries and How to Catch Them

**方向**：大语言模型 · 👍 2 | **作者**：Aditi Patodiya | [原文](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05881) | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05881)

<sub>英文标题：Broken on Arrival: Non-Functional Quantized LLM Artifacts in Public Model Registries and How to Catch Them</sub>

> Developers download quantized large language models (LLMs) from public registries to run them locally. None of the registries we examined documented functional testing of their files. We tested all 305 eligible code-capable GGUF tags (the llama.cpp format) across 15 model lines in the official Ollama library, plus 22 popular Hugging Face community files. A 15-task code screen identified suspects, which we retested on all 164 HumanEval+ tasks. Seven official tags (2.3%) solved at most six of them. All were low-bit quantizations of models with at most four billion parameters. We rebuilt five suspect files from the original weights. All five registry files matched our uncalibrated rebuilds tensor for tensor, so none was corrupted. Exact reproducibility did not imply function. With the weights, toolchain and tensor mix held fixed, adding an importance matrix raised three Qwen2.5-Coder-3B builds from zero to 76-113 solved tasks. The same uncalibrated recipe left the smaller Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B functional, showing that the recipe alone does not predict failure. None of 66 sampled tags that passed the screen was non-functional. We release the audit data and the quantcheck tool and recommend screening low-bit tags before publication, then calibrating or withholding failures.

## 多模态（1 篇）

### ⭐⭐⭐ OpenFlyV2: An onboard vision-language-action model for aerial perception and navigation

**方向**：多模态 · 👍 4 | **作者**：Junli Liu, Zhongrui You, Chenhui Li 等 12 人 | [原文](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39287) | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39287)

<sub>英文标题：OpenFlyV2: An onboard vision-language-action model for aerial perception and navigation</sub>

> Human cognition flexibly draws on a shared perceptual foundation to understand environments, attend to referenced entities, reason about spatial relationships, and act. Endowing aerial robots with an analogous functional organization remains challenging, as existing systems are often specialized for individual capabilities, dependent on offboard computation, or unable to sustain closed-loop operation on resource-constrained platforms. We present OpenFlyV2, a unified onboard vision-language-action architecture that uses a shared vision-language representation to support semantic understanding, language-grounded perception, spatial reasoning, and continuous aerial control within a single model. A compact action expert converts the shared multimodal context into short-horizon flight waypoints, while motion-aware temporal memory and action priors reduce the computational cost of closed-loop execution. We construct , a simulation-based dataset comprising approximately 70,000 navigation trajectories and 60,000 vision-language instruction--response pairs. OpenFlyV2 generates action chunks, each comprising 12 waypoints, entirely onboard at 10 Hz using an NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX. It transfers zero-shot from simulation to physical quadrotor flight, achieving a 48\% navigation success rate across four real-world environments without real-world aerial training data. Extensive results demonstrate that broad vision-language competence and continuous closed-loop flight can coexist within a single model on a resource-constrained aerial platform. Code will be made publicly available at https://github.com/Ideal-ljl/AeroBrain.
